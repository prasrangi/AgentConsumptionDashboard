    How the numbers are worked out, and what is assumed
    • Revenue is the Copilot Credits the agents use, priced on the purchase option chosen. It is the figure the calculator workbook calls monthly cost. Pay-as-you-go is — a credit; a capacity pack is — credits for — a month.
    • Credits follow the workbook line for line: each activity's monthly volume times its credits, blended by the share of licensed users, plus the safety buffer. At the workbook's own inputs the page shows the workbook's Monthly Estimate.
    • When users change, the volumes the workbook ties to users move with them: units of work per user, knowledge questions per user and follow-up turns per unit of work. Volumes set on their own, such as Advisor drafts, tenders and autonomous runs, stay where they are set. The table of activities shows which is which.
    • In the forecast users grow by the chosen rate at the start of each year, and all usage grows with them, including the volumes set on their own. Platform overhead (polling flows, harness build sessions and test runs) stays flat. A year's revenue is twelve months at that year's volume.
    • Packs are bought for the whole portfolio, so with a pack option each agent's revenue is its share of the portfolio's bill by credits.
    • Planned agents count from the year they go live, as users times the credits per user you entered, plus the buffer.
    • Volumes are illustrative until real figures replace them: the workbook marks sellers, quotes, Advisor drafts, licence share and the other-scenario volumes that way.
    • Not counted, as in the workbook: Dataverse storage, Power Automate licences, Azure costs and the Microsoft Copilot licences themselves.
